Lesson 01 / 23

A motor converts energy into motion

Physical Intuition / Mental Model

A motor gives the shaft a turning push, while the rotor and its load resist changes in motion. Torque describes that push, speed describes how fast the shaft turns, and position describes where it has reached.

By the end: Distinguish torque, speed and position.

The stator stays still; the rotor turns. Magnetic fields produce a turning effect called torque, measured in newton-metres (N·m). Speed tells you how quickly angle changes. Position tells you where the shaft is. A heavy rotor has more inertia: the same torque changes its speed more slowly.

Think of a door: pushing near its handle produces more turning effect than the same push near its hinge. Use that lever-arm idea first, then convert shaft speed before calculating power.

1 · A perpendicular force produces torque through its lever arm.

τ = F · r = 1 N × 0.02 m = 0.02 N·m

τ
Torque [N·m].
F
Perpendicular force [N].
r
Distance from shaft axis to where the force acts [m].

2 · Convert revolutions per minute to radians per second.

ω = 2π · n / 60 = 62.83 rad/s

ω
Mechanical angular speed [rad/s].
n
Shaft speed: 600 RPM = 10 revolutions/s here.
2π, 60
2π radians/revolution and 60 seconds/minute are the conversion factors.

3 · Turning force multiplied by angular speed gives mechanical power.

Pshaft = τ · ω ≈ 1.26 W

Pshaft
Mechanical shaft power [W].
τ
Torque: 0.02 N·m here.
ω
Angular speed: 62.83 rad/s here.

Bench observation · Identify the actuator

  1. With power disconnected, identify the two wires of your brushed motor and the separate power/signal connections on a servo. Do not infer a servo pinout from wire position alone.
  2. Draw three columns: quantity, unit, instrument. Fill in position/degrees, speed/RPM, voltage/V and current/A.
  3. Calculate mechanical power for 0.02 N·m at 600 RPM: multiply torque by 62.83 rad/s.

Record: 1.26 W mechanical output; electrical input must also cover losses.

If it differs: Do not multiply N·m by RPM directly. Convert RPM to rad/s first.

Check yourself: Does twice the speed always mean twice the torque?

No. Torque and speed are separate quantities. The motor’s voltage, current, load and operating limits determine their relationship.

Common Bench Trap

Mistaking RPM for torque can make a fast, lightly loaded motor look capable of a much heavier job. Record load and speed separately, and use rad/s when multiplying speed by torque to calculate watts.

Lesson 02 / 23

The spinning motor pushes back electrically

Physical Intuition / Mental Model

As a motor spins, it also acts like a generator whose voltage pushes against the supply. Loading the shaft slows that generator action, letting more current flow and producing more turning force.

By the end: Explain startup current and back EMF.

In a brushed motor, brushes and a segmented commutator switch rotor-coil connections as it turns. This keeps useful torque in the desired direction. Rotation also generates a voltage called back electromotive force, or back EMF. It opposes the applied voltage in normal motoring operation.

1 · At steady current, the supply balances copper drop and generated voltage.

V = R · I + E
I = (V − E) / R

V
Applied motor voltage [V].
R
Winding resistance [Ω].
I
Winding current [A].
E
Back EMF opposing the supply [V].

2 · Motion generates back EMF; winding current generates torque.

E = Ke · ω
τ = Kt · I

E
Back EMF [V].
Ke
Back-EMF constant [V·s/rad].
ω
Mechanical angular speed [rad/s]; at startup ω = 0, so E = 0.
τ
Electromagnetic torque [N·m].
Kt
Torque constant [N·m/A].
I
Winding current [A].

3 · Current heats the winding even when the shaft is stopped.

Pcopper = I² · R

Pcopper
Resistive winding heat [W].
I
Winding current [A].
R
Winding resistance [Ω].

Hypothetical example: a 6 V motor with 12 Ω winding resistance draws about 0.5 A when stationary if inductive transients and brush drops are ignored. Once E reaches 4.8 V, current is about (6−4.8)/12 = 0.1 A. These are invented teaching parameters, not specifications of your motor.

Add load and the shaft initially slows. Lower back EMF allows more current; more current produces more torque. A fixed voltage therefore does not maintain exactly fixed speed. If the motor cannot move, high current can continue heating the winding.

Paper lab · Predict a load change

  1. Use the hypothetical R = 12 Ω, V = 6 V motor. Calculate current at E = 0, 3 and 4.8 V.
  2. Calculate winding heat I²R at each point.
  3. Write why a driver chosen only from unloaded current may be too small.

Record: 0.5, 0.25, 0.1 A; 3, 0.75, 0.12 W winding heat.

Bench Guardrail

This is a paper calculation. Do not stall your motor to reproduce it: obtain its current specification or characterize it with a suitable current-limited setup before choosing a driver.

Check yourself: Why does starting require more current than free running?

Initially there is no speed-generated back EMF. Winding inductance limits the initial rise, but the current can rise toward a much larger value than the running current.

Common Bench Trap

A motor that spins freely on the bench may still trip the supply or overheat its driver at startup. Check starting-current capability before connecting it; the unloaded running current does not size the driver.

Lesson 03 / 23

PWM changes on-time, not pulse height

Physical Intuition / Mental Model

Imagine pushing a playground roundabout with many short, repeated shoves: longer shoves deliver more average effort without making each shove stronger. PWM similarly changes how long a voltage is applied, while inductance and inertia smooth the resulting current and motion.

By the end: Measure frequency and duty on a real STM32 pin.

Pulse-width modulation repeatedly switches a signal on and off. Keep three questions separate: how high is each pulse, how often does it repeat, and what fraction of the time is it high?

1 · Period and frequency describe repetition.

f = 1 / T
1 / (0.001 s) = 1000 Hz

f
Repetition frequency [Hz].
T
Time for one complete period [s]; 1 ms here.

2 · Duty is the high-time fraction, not the voltage height.

D = thigh / T = 250 µs / 1000 µs = 0.25 = 25%

D
Duty fraction [dimensionless], or 100D in percent.
thigh
High interval [s, or the same time unit used for T].
T
Full period [s, or the same time unit used for thigh].

A transistor used as a switch can spend most of its time fully on or fully off, reducing losses compared with deliberately dropping voltage across it. It still has conduction and switching losses. Motor inductance smooths current; inertia smooths speed. Neither makes the winding voltage constant.

The STM32 output stays near 0/3.3 V as duty changes. A separate driver switches the motor supply. For an ideal winding waveform switching between 0 and 6 V, 25% duty averages 1.5 V. This alone does not specify current or RPM.

Lesson 04 / 23

A half-bridge selects a phase voltage

Physical Intuition / Mental Model

Think of a phase terminal as a junction with one controlled path to the positive supply and another to ground. A half-bridge chooses which path drives that junction, while the motor and diodes determine what happens when both paths are off.

By the end: Name the two switches and the phase node.

A half-bridge places a high-side switch between the positive bus and a phase node, and a low-side switch between that node and supply ground. With high-side on and low-side off, the node is driven upward. With low-side on and high-side off, it is driven downward. Both off leaves the node undriven by the switches, although diodes and the load can still determine its voltage.

A power MOSFET responds to the voltage between its gate and source. When an N-channel high-side source rises with the phase node, its gate drive must rise with it; a gate driver provides that controlled relationship.

Bench Guardrail

Use these bridge diagrams to reason about switching functions. An STM32 GPIO is a logic command, not a universal power-MOSFET gate supply; do not construct the depicted power inverter on a solderless breadboard.

Trace · The left leg of the bridge below

  1. Find AH (high side), AL (low side) and phase A in Lesson 7’s bridge SVG.
  2. Write its allowed command pairs: 10 high, 01 low, 00 undriven.
  3. Circle the forbidden 11 pair and follow its supply-to-ground path.

Record: A three-state command table and the bypass path that does not pass through the motor.

If it differs: An undriven node is not guaranteed to be at 0 V; inductive current can drive it to a diode clamp.

Check yourself: What reference defines the motor terminal voltage?

Voltage always lies between two nodes. Phase-to-ground voltage differs from the voltage across two motor terminals.

Common Bench Trap

Treating an undriven phase as ground creates a false current-path calculation and can lead to an unsafe probe connection. Name both voltage-reference nodes first; the LA1010 stays on the stated logic signals.

Lesson 05 / 23

Give one switch time to turn off

Physical Intuition / Mental Model

Two people passing through a narrow doorway need a small pause so the first clears it before the second enters. Complementary power switches need the same handover pause because an off command does not stop conduction instantly.

By the end: Capture both dead-time intervals.

Shoot-through occurs when both switches in one leg conduct, creating a low-resistance path across the supply. A real switch takes time to turn off. Dead time delays the opposite switch turning on so the previous one can finish turning off.

STM32 TIM1 can generate complementary signals with built-in dead time. In our active-high demonstration both outputs are low during the gap. Adding too much gap also changes the delivered voltage and diode conduction, so it is not a setting to maximize blindly.

Lab B · Capture non-overlap

  1. Use the two-output SVG; the two LED branches are optional visual indicators. Set LAB_MODE = 1.
  2. Capture CH0 and CH1 at 10 MS/s or higher for about 2 ms.
  3. Zoom into PA8 falling → PA7 rising and PA7 falling → PA8 rising. Measure both gaps.
  4. Check that no captured sample has both signals high. Save a zoomed capture of each edge.

Record: About 2 µs each; at 10 MS/s this spans about 20 sample intervals.

If it differs: Check sample rate, channel labels and output polarities. The reference duty is 50%, but active pulses are shortened by dead time; do not expect exactly 500 µs high on each pin.

Go deeper · Why the dead time does not use the 1 MHz counter tick

With 16 MHz timer input and CKD = 00, tDTS = 62.5 ns. In the first DTG range, delay = DTG × tDTS, so DTG = 32 gives 2 µs. PSC = 15 separately makes the counting rate 1 MHz. ARR = 999 gives a 1 ms period. Larger DTG encodings are nonlinear; consult BDTR in RM0390.

Check yourself: Does a clean GPIO capture prove a real inverter cannot shoot through?

No. Gate-driver propagation delays, MOSFET switching times, wiring and faults still need evaluation. This lab validates logic timing only.

Common Bench Trap

A neat two-microsecond logic gap can coexist with overlapping power-device conduction. Treat this capture as a check of the timer outputs; an actual inverter also needs its driver delays and switching behavior verified.

Lesson 06 / 23

Turning off voltage does not turn off current

Physical Intuition / Mental Model

Current in a winding has continuity much like motion in a heavy flywheel: removing the drive does not instantly remove the stored energy. When a switch opens, that current finds another path, and the voltage changes to support its flow.

By the end: Trace the inductive off-time path.

Recall Module 1: an inductor stores energy, ½LI². Its current cannot jump instantly. When a bridge changes state, winding current transfers to a diode or another conducting switch. This is recirculation. The winding voltage can change quickly while current remains continuous.

In a low-side switch with a flyback diode, current can circulate through the winding and diode after turn-off. In a bridge, body diodes and deliberately enabled switches provide different paths. Slow decay applies a relatively small opposing winding voltage; fast decay applies a larger opposing voltage. The names describe current decay, not the MCU’s execution speed.

Reason · Follow A-to-B current

  1. In the full bridge, begin with AH and BL on and current from A to B.
  2. Command all switches off: current can initially flow ground → AL body diode → A → winding → B → BH body diode → bus. Energy can return to the bus.
  3. Compare both lows on: A/B are near ground; current circulates through the low-side devices and winding.

Record: A fast-return path and a low-voltage circulation path, with arrow direction.

If it differs: The direction of existing current selects diode conduction. “All gates off” is not the same as “all circuit current zero.”

Check yourself: Why can coast mode still contain current immediately after switching?

Stored magnetic energy needs a path. Current decays through available diode paths before the electrically unloaded shaft coasts.

Common Bench Trap

Opening the switch without a valid recirculation path can produce a voltage spike instead of an immediate stop. Trace the existing current through a complete loop before changing a power-stage switching state.

Lesson 07 / 23

A full bridge reverses the motor voltage

Physical Intuition / Mental Model

A motor responds to the voltage difference between its two terminals, so moving either end changes the electrical push. A full bridge controls both ends to drive forward, reverse polarity, connect the ends for braking, or release them for coasting.

By the end: Distinguish forward, reverse, brake and coast.

Two half-bridges drive the two terminals of a brushed motor. AH plus BL puts A above B; BH plus AL reverses the polarity. These are diagonal switch pairs. Driving both low switches connects both motor terminals near ground and can brake the rotor. Turning all switches off commands coast, after the current transient discussed above.

Bench Guardrail

Reversal while spinning can make the supply and back EMF combine to produce large current. In the optional motor lab, release the button, disconnect motor power and wait for the shaft to stop before changing direction.

Lesson 08 / 23

BLDC moves commutation into the electronics

Physical Intuition / Mental Model

A brushed motor carries its switching mechanism on the shaft, so the coils change connection as the rotor moves. A BLDC motor asks external electronics to perform that handover in step with rotor position.

By the end: Explain how a three-phase motor differs from your brushed motor.

In a brushless DC motor, permanent magnets are typically on the rotor and coils are on the stator. An electronic inverter changes which coils carry current. The supply is DC, but the winding voltages and currents change with time. “DC” does not mean connect one phase permanently to a battery.

The controller must energize the coils in relation to rotor position. Hall sensors can identify rotor sectors; sensorless systems estimate position from electrical signals. We focus on Hall-based six-step commutation. A complete hobby servo contains its own motor, gearing, position sensor and controller. Sending it a command does not expose its internal commutation.

Identify · Three different systems

  1. Draw your brushed motor as a two-terminal load plus internal mechanical commutation.
  2. Draw the servo as power + command entering an internal controller/gearbox/sensor assembly.
  3. Draw the BLDC as three phase terminals plus optional Hall wiring connected to an external inverter.

Record: Three block diagrams with power and feedback paths marked.

If it differs: Three servo wires are not three motor phases. A two-wire fan may contain internal electronics; here your motor is explicitly identified as brushed.

Check yourself: Can the 1203B drive a bare three-phase BLDC motor?

No. It is a brushed-motor PWM controller, not a three-phase rotor-position-dependent commutator.

Common Bench Trap

Connecting three servo wires as though they were BLDC phases confuses power, ground and command. Identify the actuator type and its documented terminal functions before wiring any controller.

Lesson 09 / 23

Count pole pairs before counting speed

Physical Intuition / Mental Model

A patterned wheel can repeat its pattern several times during one physical turn. The shaft angle counts the turn itself, while electrical angle counts each repeating north–south magnetic pattern.

By the end: Convert mechanical angle and electrical angle.

Mechanical angle describes the physical shaft. Electrical angle describes where the magnetic pattern is within one repeating field cycle. A rotor with eight poles has four north/south pairs, so p = 4. One shaft revolution then contains four electrical revolutions.

1 · Each pole pair repeats the magnetic pattern once per shaft turn.

θe = p · θm

θe

p
Number of north–south pole pairs [dimensionless count].
θm
Mechanical shaft angle [mechanical degrees or radians]; use the same angle unit on both sides.

2 · Convert shaft revolutions per minute into electrical cycles per second.

fe

p
Pole-pair count [dimensionless].
n
Shaft speed [RPM = revolutions/min].
60
Seconds per minute [s/min].

Δθe = 360° / 6 = 60°
Δθm = 60° / p

Δθe
Sector width [electrical degrees].
Δθm

p

No. It turned 60/4 = 15 mechanical degrees.

Lesson 10 / 23

Three legs select a winding pair

Physical Intuition / Mental Model

Picture three roads meeting at a central junction inside the motor. In a basic six-step sector, current enters by one winding and leaves by another, while the third road is not actively driven.

By the end: Describe the third phase in basic six-step operation.

A three-phase inverter has legs A, B and C. Each leg has a high-side and a low-side switch. In the basic two-phase-on six-step pattern, one phase sources current, another sinks it and the third is undriven. For A+ / B−, AH and BL are selected; C is floating.

The motor’s windings, not shown inside the switch blocks, complete the circuit. In a star-connected motor, current travels through two windings via their shared neutral. The neutral is generally not connected to supply ground. That difference matters when writing a phase-voltage equation.

Trace · Use the interactive bridge in Lesson 12

  1. Find the selected high-side and low-side switch for sector 1.
  2. Trace bus → AH → phase A winding → neutral → phase B winding → BL → ground.
  3. Confirm both C switches are off and no leg has both switches selected.

Record: A valid path through two windings; the third phase undriven.

If it differs: A colored gate command indicates a requested switch state. It does not show the actual current during commutation or dead time.

Check yourself: Why are six switches needed even though only two phases conduct in a sector?

Each of the three phase terminals must be able to source, sink or stop actively driving as the rotor moves through different sectors.

Common Bench Trap

Jumpering the star neutral to supply ground changes the circuit and invalidates the intended current paths. In this model the neutral is isolated; trace through two windings without adding a ground connection.

Lesson 11 / 23

Three Hall bits tell you a sector

Physical Intuition / Mental Model

Three Hall signals act like a coarse magnetic signpost telling the controller which rotor sector it is in. Their bits describe position information; they do not carry the current that turns the motor.

By the end: Read H1/H2/H3 without confusing them with phase outputs.

A Hall sensor detects magnetic field. Three suitably placed sensors produce a changing three-bit code as the rotor turns. In the example used here, the six codes are 001, 101, 100, 110, 010, 011. Each adjacent code changes one bit. This is our declared 120° Hall convention; it is not a universal connector or phase-wiring standard.

Physical Intuition / Mental Model

Commutation is a timed handover: as the rotor moves into the next sector, another winding pair takes over the job of pulling it onward. The handover works only when the sensor sequence and the selected winding pair describe the same physical rotor position.

By the end: Step through a six-state sequence and reject an invalid input.

Commutation means changing which winding pair is energized. The teaching table below defines sector boundaries and one phase/Hall alignment together. A real motor may use different wire colors, Hall bit order and alignment; blindly copying the table can produce poor torque or excessive current.

Read rotor sector, select the appropriate pair, apply PWM and repeat when the next valid sector arrives. A robust implementation checks both the code and the transition: 001→100 contains two bit changes and skips a sector in this example. Check yourself: What happens if firmware adds Ki·error each sample but forgets Ts?

Its effective integral gain depends on the sample rate and can be vastly too large. Ki must be interpreted with explicit units and timing.

Common Bench Trap

Changing the control rate while omitting the sample-time factor changes the accumulated correction per second. Include the actual interval in the integral update; at a 1 ms interval, omitting it makes the numerical update 1000 times too large for the same Ki.

Lesson 23 / 23

Correction can arrive too late

Physical Intuition / Mental Model

A helpful correction becomes harmful if it arrives after the motion has already swung the other way. Stability margins measure how much extra gain or lag a stated linear feedback model can tolerate before this reinforcing behavior reaches the instability boundary.

By the end: Interpret loop margins without treating them as guarantees.

Negative feedback corrects error, but enough lag can make correction reinforce the deviation. Stability asks whether small deviations decay in the stated model. A bounded motor command or a clipped graph does not prove stable regulation; a limited loop can sustain oscillation.

Use the loop transfer to judge how a correction returns around the feedback path. Use the closed-loop transfer to predict what the output does after that correction is connected. The distinction keeps the controller and measurement path visible in the calculation.

1 · Include controller, plant and measurement in the loop product.

Check yourself: Why should you evaluate margin on the open loop while caring about the closed loop?

The feedback characteristic equation contains 1+L(s). The loop’s gain and phase show how close it comes to making that denominator vanish; the resulting time response belongs to the closed loop.

Common Bench Trap

A saturated command or clipped graph can hide growing oscillation without fixing its cause. Inspect loop gain and phase under the stated model, then check the sampled, limited time response and real timing separately.
