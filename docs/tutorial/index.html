How to learn here

Picture it. Predict it. Prove it.

Build an intuition for what the circuit will do before reaching for an equation or a jumper wire. Each lesson follows the same path, so you can spend your attention on the idea you are learning.

  1. 01 · Picture the physicsStart with a two-sentence mental model. Describe what moves, stores energy or changes over time.
  2. 02 · Make a predictionFollow the annotated equations, meanings and units. Write your expected value before testing.
  3. 03 · Follow the nodesRead the Bench Guardrail, then trace the SVG and signal flow to the exact breadboard rows. Wire with power off.
  4. 04 · Explain the evidenceCompare the result with your prediction. Answer the self-check before opening its answer and Common Bench Trap.

Your running project is a small controller test bench: first an LED load, then measured signals and simulated feedback, and later a compatible low-power motor with a suitable assembled driver. Use the labeled paper route when a component or instrument is missing; record the distinction between a prediction and a measurement.

Bench Guardrail · Motor power

Use the breadboard for low-current signals and verified teaching circuits. Real BLDC operation needs a compatible assembled driver, supply and known motor; a breadboard cannot reproduce an inverter’s power layout. A generic hobby ESC may hide commutation or lack the signals needed by these labs. Confirm the motor and driver interfaces before selecting power hardware.

Choose your starting point.

Begin with Module 1 bench setup if you are still learning how a breadboard, GPIO and return path fit together. Once you can build and explain its input/output capstone, continue to Module 2: motion and feedback. Keep a prediction and a result for every experiment in your local notebook.
